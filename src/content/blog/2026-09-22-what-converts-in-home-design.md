## The research, put together

Three studies fed into this, each looking at a different part of the same
funnel: a competitive analysis of home-design and floor-planning products,
an SEO audit of how people actually search for this category, and a
conversion review of our own path from ad or search result to a finished
design.

Individually, each is a fairly ordinary study. It's the overlap that's the
finding.

## What the competitive scan found

Every competitor evaluated requires some form of commitment before you can
touch the actual design surface — an account, an email for a "free trial,"
a waitlist, or a scheduled call with sales. Some categories of tools
(the higher-end, contractor-facing ones especially) treat this as
non-negotiable, since their business model depends on the lead. None of
them offer a genuinely zero-signup path into a working 3D editor.

## What the SEO and conversion data said

Search terms in this category skew toward people trying to *see* something
concrete before they commit to anything — "floor plan tool free,"
"design my house online," phrasing that reads as evaluation, not
purchase intent. Our own conversion data showed the same pattern from the
other side: the biggest drop-off in the existing funnel happened at the
first point we asked for anything — an email, an account — before the
visitor had gotten to experience the actual product.

## What the app audit confirmed

The app audit (see [Screenshot Capture](/diary/blog/2026-09-19-screenshot-capture/))
independently confirmed the one thing that made this actionable rather than
just an observation: `app.buildmy.house` already doesn't require any of
that. The editor opens and works with nothing gated. The gap wasn't in the
product — it was that nothing in our own marketing said this out loud.

## The positioning that emerged

Put together, this is what became the [homepage decision](/diary/blog/2026-09-10-the-product-direction/):
lead with "no download, no signup," because it's not just a nicer way to say
"try it for free" — it's a claim none of our competitors can make about
their own product without changing their business model first.
